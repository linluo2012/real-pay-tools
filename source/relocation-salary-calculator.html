Common questions

How do I work out an equivalent salary in a more expensive city?

Multiply by the ratio of the two indices. If you earn $100,000 where the index is 100 and move somewhere indexed at 130, the same standard of living costs roughly $130,000. Tax then shifts the answer again, so the cleanest method is to compare take-home pay divided by the local index rather than comparing gross salaries.

Is a higher salary in a big city actually better?

Often not. Housing drives most of the difference and does not scale linearly with income: a 30% higher salary where rent is 80% higher leaves you worse off. State and local income tax can remove another several percent. What shows the real answer is take-home pay measured against local costs, not the gross figure.

How accurate are cost-of-living indices?

Indicative, not precise. Published indices use different baskets of goods and different base cities and will not match your actual spending. Someone who rents and never drives experiences a very different difference from a homeowner with two cars. Treat an index as a starting point and override it with your own research on rent, transport and childcare.

Should I count state income tax when comparing cities?

Yes. The gap between a no-income-tax state and a high-tax state can be worth several thousand dollars a year on a median salary, and some cities add a local tax on top. Because it applies to the whole salary it is often a larger swing than expected, and it stacks on top of the cost difference.
