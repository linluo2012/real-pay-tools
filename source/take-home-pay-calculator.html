Common questions

What is the difference between marginal and effective tax rate?

Your marginal rate is the rate applied to your last dollar of income — the bracket your taxable income falls into. Your effective rate is total tax divided by total income, and it is always lower because your first dollars are taxed at 0%, 10% and 12%. A single filer on $75,000 in 2026 sits in the 22% bracket but pays an effective federal rate near 10.2%. Confusing the two is the most common reason people overestimate how much tax a raise will cost them.

Does contributing to a 401(k) reduce Social Security and Medicare tax?

No. Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce federal and state income tax, but Social Security and Medicare tax is still calculated on full gross wages. This calculator follows that rule, so FICA is applied to gross pay. Health premiums paid through a Section 125 cafeteria plan are treated differently and do reduce FICA wages, which is why two people on the same salary can have different FICA amounts.

Why is my actual paycheck different from this estimate?

Payroll withholding depends on your Form W-4 entries, including the two-jobs box, dependents and any extra withholding you request. It also depends on post-tax deductions such as Roth contributions, union dues and garnishments, none of which a general calculator can know. Treat this as a strong estimate of annual tax, compare it against a paystub, and adjust your W-4 if the gap is large.

Which states have no income tax?

As of 2026, Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming levy no tax on wages. New Hampshire taxes only interest and dividend income above a threshold, not earned wages. Moving from a state with a 6% effective rate to one of these on the same salary is equivalent to a raise of roughly that percentage, though it is often partly offset by higher property tax, sales tax or housing cost.

How accurate is the state tax figure?

It is an approximation. Most states use graduated brackets, and several cities and counties add their own tax on top. The rates used here are representative effective rates for a middle-income filer, not the exact statutory schedule. Use the figure to compare offers between states, and check your state revenue department for the amount that applies to you.
